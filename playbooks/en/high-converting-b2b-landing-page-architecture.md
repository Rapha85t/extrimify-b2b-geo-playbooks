> **Answer capsule:** High-converting B2B landing page architecture matches buyer intent with a clear offer, credible proof, useful qualification, and a low-friction next step. The goal is not simply to collect form submissions. It is to help buyers research confidently, route qualified prospects to the right conversation, and measure whether those meetings create pipeline.

## What belongs above the fold on a B2B landing page?

Make the first screen answer three questions: who is this for, what problem does it solve, and what should the visitor do next? Use a specific headline, a concrete benefit, a brief supporting line, and one primary call to action. Keep the promise consistent with the ad, search intent, or audience that brought the visitor there.

Add a nearby trust cue, such as relevant customer names or a substantiated outcome. Avoid crowding the opening with every product detail. Extrimify’s [BOOST program](https://extrimify.com/en/boost) runs for 4 to 8 weeks, depending on scope and objectives.

## Which CTA is more likely to generate qualified meetings?

Use language that sets expectations: “Book a fit call,” “Request a demo,” or “Get a tailored assessment.” Match the CTA to the buyer’s readiness and the complexity of the offer. A generic “Submit” button tells visitors little about what happens next.

Support buyers who prefer to research independently with enough information to assess fit before talking to sales. Where appropriate, offer calendar booking immediately after the form. Explore our [B2B approach](https://extrimify.com/en/linkedin-b2b).

## How many fields should a B2B landing page form have?

There is no universal ideal. Include only fields that improve personalization or routing. Name, business email, and role may be enough to start; ask about company size, industry, or need only when the answers affect meeting quality. Ask about budget or urgency when the context makes those questions useful.

You can qualify in stages: collect a few details first, then present follow-up questions or available meeting times. Evaluate the share of leads that fit your customer profile, not just the form completion rate.

## Which proof reduces buyer risk before a sales meeting?

Choose evidence that addresses the buyer’s concerns: relevant case studies, metrics with context, recognizable customer logos, testimonials, or security information. Explain what the meeting covers and what the prospect can expect, so the next step feels useful rather than risky.

## How do you measure landing-page meeting quality?

Connect page events to GA4 and your CRM: visits, CTA clicks, form submissions, booked meetings, attendance, qualification, and pipeline progression. Retain campaign and page-version data so you can compare audience and message performance.
