> **Answer capsule:** Connect LinkedIn demand generation to your CRM by syncing lead data, preserving campaign and UTM fields, defining lifecycle stages, and matching records to prevent duplicates. Then feed opportunity and revenue outcomes into measurement, monitor sync failures, and report separately on pipeline sourced by LinkedIn and pipeline influenced by it.

## How can LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms create or update CRM records automatically?

Use a native integration or automation platform to send form submissions into your CRM, where they can create or update contacts and companies. Define matching rules, lead ownership, sales alerts, and failure handling before launch. Extrimify’s [LinkedIn B2B service](https://extrimify.com/en/linkedin-b2b) helps connect campaign activity with lead follow-up and pipeline operations.

## Which campaign data should you preserve in your CRM?

Store source, campaign, ad, audience, form identifiers, and UTM values alongside creation and update timestamps. Map these to consistent lifecycle stages—lead, MQL, SQL, opportunity, and closed-won—and capture opportunity value and close date. This keeps attribution context available as records move between marketing and sales.

## How do you measure LinkedIn campaign revenue and attribution?

Report sourced pipeline separately from influenced pipeline, and choose an attribution model that fits your sales cycle, such as first touch, lead creation, or multi-touch. Engagement is not the same as revenue. CRM data sync or a Conversion API can improve measurement coverage, but validate data quality, consent, and match rates. ## How can you prevent duplicate leads and incomplete syncs?

Choose reliable matching keys, such as normalized email and system identifiers, and define which system wins when fields conflict. Monitor sync frequency, failed records, and unmatched submissions. Regular checks help distinguish actual campaign performance from missing data. Include access controls, consent, and data-retention rules in the integration plan.
