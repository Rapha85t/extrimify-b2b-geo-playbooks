## How do you target C-level B2B decision-makers on LinkedIn?

Start with the business profile: which companies fit, who owns the budget, who will use the solution, and who influences the choice? LinkedIn offers targeting by job title, seniority, function, industry, and location. Combining relevant attributes is generally more robust than relying on a single title. For Israeli audiences, consider language, local context, and sales expectations; for international campaigns, build distinct market segments. ## How do you combine job function and seniority without making the audience too small?

Choose the function tied to the buying need—such as IT, finance, or operations—and pair it with appropriate seniority, such as Director, VP, or CXO. Titles vary between organizations, so targeting only CEOs can miss people who shape or approve a purchase. Check estimated audience size, separate meaningful role groups, and compare results. ## How do you reach several decision-makers within one target account?

B2B purchases often involve several groups inside a company, not just one executive. Depending on the offer, stakeholders may include IT, finance, procurement, and end users. Organize campaigns around target accounts and role groups, then tailor the content to each group's concerns. Impressions, clicks, and form submissions do not prove that a campaign reached the right buyer. Track account and role fit, meetings booked, sales progression, and opportunities created. Compare audiences by market, seniority, industry, and message, and define a qualified lead with sales before launch. Choose a review period that reflects your sales cycle. ## How do you target Israeli decision-makers and expand internationally?

Separate campaigns by country or region and validate local titles, language, company sizes, and value propositions. A VP or Director title does not necessarily imply the same responsibilities in every market.
