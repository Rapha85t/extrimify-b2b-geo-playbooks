> **Answer capsule:** In week one, decide which customer segment and value proposition matter most, then define one outcome metric and a few indicators that explain it. Review qualified meetings, sales acceptance, and pipeline movement weekly, not just impressions, clicks, or raw lead volume.

## What should you decide first, and what can you ignore?

The first decision is not which channel to advertise on. Decide what kind of deal you want to create, for whom, and what counts as a qualified opportunity. Agree with sales on a focused customer profile, business problem, clear offer, and the criteria for moving a lead into an opportunity. If the team disagrees on what an opportunity means, even a polished dashboard will produce numbers that do not help prioritize.

Choose one main outcome metric, such as qualified opportunities created or new pipeline value, plus a small set of leading indicators. At the beginning, set aside metrics disconnected from a decision: follower growth, impressions alone, click-through rate without context, or form fills without qualification. These can help diagnose performance, but they do not prove growth.

Marketing analytics guidance emphasizes connecting activity to conversions and business outcomes. Resources such as [HubSpot’s marketing analytics guide](https://www.hubspot.com/glossary/marketing-analytics) and [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs) help distinguish reach, engagement, and business progress. Do not assume an external benchmark automatically gives your company the right target.

## Which B2B marketing KPIs should you track?

Start with a simple funnel: target accounts or people reached, meaningful responses or visits, conversations held, leads accepted by sales, opportunities created, pipeline value, and eventually closed deals. Not every company needs to measure every stage at the same frequency. Adapt measurement to sales-cycle length and activity volume.

Write an operational definition for each stage. A qualified lead, for example, is a person at a company that meets agreed criteria, not everyone who submits an email address. An opportunity is a conversation with a need, a relevant stakeholder, and an agreed next step, not simply an introductory call. Shared definitions make it possible to calculate stage conversion, spot friction, and improve a specific step instead of blindly increasing activity.

Alongside outcomes, track response time, sales acceptance rate, meetings held, opportunity conversion, and opportunity source. Calculate acquisition cost or return on investment only when cost and revenue data are reliable, and state the limitations. B2B purchases often include several touchpoints and multiple people at one account, so assigning all revenue to one channel can give an incomplete picture. See [HubSpot’s overview of B2B marketing analytics](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/b2b-marketing-analytics).

## What should happen in the first 30, 60, and 90 days?

Days 1 to 30: diagnose the market, offer, data, and handoff to sales. Review the CRM, lead sources, forms, tagging, and response time. Select one audience and agree on shared definitions for lead, qualified lead, and opportunity. [Strategic Diagnosis](https://extrimify.com/en/strategic-diagnosis) helps identify gaps before scaling activity; the process takes 2 to 4 weeks.

Days 31 to 60: build a repeatable process around a message, content asset, landing page or conversation path, and CRM follow-up. Connect [LinkedIn](https://extrimify.com/en/linkedin-b2b) activity to a sales process, not just reactions. In parallel, strengthen useful content so it can be understood and surfaced in AI-powered search responses, or GEO, without claiming guaranteed visibility in a particular engine.

Days 61 to 90: review which messages and channels generated relevant conversations and pipeline progress. Stop activity that creates volume without quality, improve the weakest conversion step, and expand only when the signals support it. The [BOOST system](https://extrimify.com/en/boost) runs for 4 to 8 weeks and supports implementation of a structured marketing system once the direction is clear. This is a sequence of work, not a promise of results.

## How do diagnosis, BOOST, LinkedIn, and GEO connect to pipeline?

Diagnosis clarifies the decisions: audience, offer, sales process, measurement, and priorities. BOOST turns those decisions into implementation and a regular operating rhythm. LinkedIn supports reaching relevant people and accounts through content and professional conversations. GEO improves the chance that clear, useful content is understood and found in AI-based search interfaces. None of these replaces a shared opportunity definition, a clean CRM, or collaboration with sales.

Give each component a metric tied to an outcome. On LinkedIn, look at relevant conversations and meetings, not engagement alone. For GEO, track appearances, referrals, and qualified visits where measurable, along with conversations that mention AI search. In the CRM, review acceptance, stage movement, and pipeline value. Explain what was directly attributed and what may have contributed to the journey. This avoids double-counting and reports that imply causation without evidence.

## What can you verify within seven days?

Run a short baseline check. Review ten recent inquiries, or all inquiries from the week if that better fits your volume. Confirm that each has a source, date, status, owner, audience fit, and next step. Count how many sales accepted, how many conversations happened, and how many became opportunities. If volume is too low, inspect leading activity indicators such as relevant responses, service-page visits, and answered inquiries.

Record the starting point, change one thing, and review again after seven days. For example, sharpen the offer or improve response time, but do not change both at once. Compare response or acceptance rates, while avoiding claims of a trend based on a tiny sample. A weekly check should reveal a data gap, operational obstacle, or early signal, not prove that a whole channel works.

Set a 30-minute weekly review: what changed, what was learned, where the funnel stalls, and what single experiment comes next. Record the decision and owner in the CRM or a shared document. The purpose is to turn KPIs into decisions, not to collect charts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which KPIs matter most in the first 90 days?

Track qualified opportunities, sales acceptance rate, meetings held, stage conversion, pipeline value, and response time. Choose only metrics that are clearly defined and actionable.

### How do you define a qualified lead and a sales opportunity?

Agree with sales on audience, need, role, and next-step criteria. Document the definitions in the CRM and check that the team applies them consistently.

### How can marketing’s contribution to pipeline be measured?

Connect source, activity, and CRM stage, then compare meetings, opportunities, and pipeline value by source. Show multiple touchpoints and avoid exclusive attribution without evidence.

### What should you check in week one without historical data?

Check data completeness and establish a baseline for source, status, fit, response time, and next step. Define provisional metrics, collect a week of data, then set realistic targets.
