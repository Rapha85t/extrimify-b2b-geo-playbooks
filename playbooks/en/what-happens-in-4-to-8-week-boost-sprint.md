> **Answer capsule:** A BOOST sprint takes 4 to 8 weeks to connect B2B marketing strategy, positioning, buyer journey, channels, content, CRM, and measurement. It moves from prioritization and planning into implementation, then closes with a documented system your team can operate and improve. Scope depends on your needs, access, and resources.

## What exactly happens during a 4-to-8-week BOOST sprint?

BOOST is a focused system-building engagement, not a disconnected list of marketing tasks. The team aligns on business goals, target buyers, and success measures, then connects strategy to messaging, buyer experience, and execution. The scope is shaped by your starting point and priorities. [Explore the BOOST service](https://extrimify.com/en/boost) or [discuss fit with the team](https://extrimify.com/en/contact).

## What is delivered in each sprint phase?

Early work maps the current setup and sharpens the audience, value proposition, and messaging. Next, those decisions inform channel priorities, content needs, workflows, and measurement. The closing phase checks how the components work together, documents what is ready, and identifies next steps. [Read about the method](https://extrimify.com/en/method).

## What can realistically launch in four weeks versus eight?

A four-week sprint usually concentrates on essential foundations and the highest-priority launch, depending on complexity and team availability. Eight weeks can provide more room to build additional assets or channels and run further checks. The agreed scope—not the calendar alone—determines what goes live, so no specific launch is guaranteed in advance.

## Does BOOST include positioning, ICP definition, and buyer-journey research?

The system plan can include defining or refining your ideal customer profile, target audiences, positioning, messaging, and buyer journey. Research depth and deliverables depend on the agreed scope. Where a deeper strategic foundation is needed, begin with a [Strategic Diagnosis](https://extrimify.com/en/strategic-diagnosis).

## How are channels and B2B content priorities chosen?

Priorities follow buyer needs, business goals, available budget, team capacity, and existing evidence—not channel popularity. Useful content helps buyers understand their problem and evaluate options on their own terms. Gartner research reports that many B2B buyers prefer a rep-free buying experience, making helpful self-service information an important consideration. Explore [B2B marketing on LinkedIn](https://extrimify.com/en/linkedin-b2b).

## Are CRM, automation, analytics, and reporting configured?

When included in the agreed scope, these tools are considered as parts of one workflow: capturing source information, routing leads, tracking activity, and reporting on agreed measures. Success metrics should be defined before implementation. The depth of configuration depends on existing tools, access, and requirements.

## What does your team need to provide, and what happens after the sprint?

The work needs timely access to relevant systems and information, input from the right stakeholders, and prompt feedback and approvals. At the end, the team reviews what is built, what is ready to activate, and what still needs a decision or resource. You can hand the system to your in-house team or explore continued support. ### FAQ

#### What happens during a 4-to-8-week BOOST sprint?

The team sets priorities, plans the system, implements agreed B2B marketing components, and documents next steps. Scope depends on your starting point and resources.

#### What can launch in four weeks?

The sprint may focus on essential foundations and one priority initiative, depending on complexity, available materials, and approvals. The launch scope is agreed during planning.
