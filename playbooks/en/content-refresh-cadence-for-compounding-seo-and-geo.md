> **Answer capsule:** Refresh priority pages every 60 to 90 days as a planning rhythm, not a mechanical rule. Start with pages tied to buyer questions and pipeline, verify what has changed, improve evidence and clarity, then measure visibility, engagement, and next-step actions. Treat AI citations as an outcome to observe, never a guaranteed result.

## Why does a 60-to-90-day content refresh cadence matter?

B2B content can become less useful even when the product has not changed. Buyers ask new questions, categories evolve, evidence gets superseded, and sales teams hear objections that were not visible when an article was published. Search engines and AI answer systems need clear, dependable material to respond to specific queries. Updating a page can help keep it accurate and helpful, but it does not guarantee a ranking, citation, or lead. The purpose is to maintain usefulness and make the page easier to evaluate, not to make a date look recent.

Freshness research needs careful interpretation. According to New Study: AI Assistants Prefer to Cite “Fresher” Content (2025), articles cited in the study’s Google AI Overview analysis had an average age of 1432 days average article age. The analysis also reported an average of 1047 days since the last update. Those figures do not prove that pages should be left untouched for years, or that every page needs an update every quarter. They show that age alone does not explain citation selection. A mature, authoritative page may still be useful when its claims remain accurate and relevant.

A separate study provides a different point of reference. According to AI Overview Content Freshness: 742 Citations (2026), the median time between a page update and citation capture was 88 days. This is an observation from a particular dataset, not a promise or universal refresh interval. Use it to justify a testable review cycle, then evaluate your own pages and audience. Google’s guidance on [core updates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates) is useful context: broad search changes are not a reason to make arbitrary edits.

## What decision should a B2B team make first, and what should it ignore?

Decide which pages support a real buying decision before commissioning new content or starting a site-wide rewrite. Select five to ten existing pages using a mix of organic impressions, clicks, visits from relevant accounts, recurring sales questions, and the role of each page in the buying journey. A page with impressions but few clicks might need a clearer promise or a more direct answer. A page that attracts the wrong audience probably needs better positioning, not more traffic.

Ignore the temptation to chase every change in an AI answer or alter a publication date without making a substantive improvement. Word count, keyword repetition, and a single observed citation are not business outcomes. Nor should a broad industry statistic outweigh evidence from your own customers. Start with one business problem: does this page help a buyer understand a problem, assess an approach, compare options, or take a sensible next step?

## How can a refresh routine connect SEO, GEO, and pipeline?

A 60-to-90-day window is a useful planning rhythm because it allows time to collect signals, learn from customer conversations, and review changes. It is not a substitute for ongoing monitoring. If a product claim, process, regulation, or key data point changes, update the relevant page when the change happens. By contrast, an evergreen explainer that remains correct may not need a full rewrite every cycle.

Review four layers during each refresh. First, check whether the core answer is still accurate and easy to find. Second, replace outdated examples, data, screenshots, or product details. Third, make the structure answer real questions in plain language. Fourth, confirm there is a natural next step, such as a diagnostic tool, a relevant service page, or a conversation. Internal links should help a reader continue, not simply decorate the article. Use [Extrimify’s method](https://extrimify.com/en/method) to treat refresh work as part of a marketing system rather than a disconnected editorial task.

The right update may be small: clarify a limitation, correct a claim, add a review date, or answer a buyer question more directly. In other cases, the positioning itself has changed and the page needs deeper work. Use [results and insights](https://extrimify.com/en/results) to define what meaningful progress means for your business: clearer messaging, relevant engagement, qualified conversations, and movement through the buying journey. Publishing more often is not automatically progress.

## What should the weekly execution sequence look like with GEO Checker?

Start with the [GEO Checker](https://extrimify.com/en/geo-checker) to establish a baseline and identify pages or questions worth investigating. Treat it as a diagnostic and monitoring aid, not as a promise that an AI system will cite a page. A short weekly sequence keeps the work practical and tied to evidence.

On day one, select one page based on visibility, commercial importance, or a repeated customer question. Record its URL in your internal tracking, the question it should answer, and the business action you want a reader to take. On day two, review search performance, site behavior, sales conversations, and relevant proposals. Identify one main gap, such as an unclear answer, stale evidence, or a missing example.

On day three, test the selected question with GEO Checker. Record the exact query, the observed answer, whether your brand is present, and any visible cited sources. Repeat checks under similar conditions where possible; one appearance is not a representative sample. On day four, fix the gap: sharpen the answer, add a credible source, include a real example, state when the material was reviewed, or add a helpful internal link. Distinguish facts, experience, and estimates so a reader can judge the evidence.

On day five, publish the revision or send it for subject-matter review. On day six, share a useful implication on LinkedIn, with context for your audience rather than a bare link. On day seven, compare early indicators with the baseline, record what changed, and decide what to investigate next. ## Which metrics can you verify within seven days?

Seven days is not enough to attribute a change in search performance to one edit, and it is certainly not enough to prove that a particular update caused an AI citation. It is enough to verify execution and inspect early signals. Save a baseline for impressions and clicks on the selected page and queries, relevant organic visits, click-through rate, engagement indicators from your analytics, and next-step actions such as visits to a service page or form submissions. Include a qualitative check: did a buyer or salesperson find the answer useful, and did it reduce the need for basic explanation?

For GEO, define a small, stable set of buyer-relevant questions and test them periodically using a consistent method. Record the date, wording, observed result, and context. Do not treat one test as representative of all users or all answer systems. Compare results over several cycles and distinguish brand presence, a mention, a source citation, referral traffic, and a qualified inquiry. A consistent [GEO Checker](https://extrimify.com/en/geo-checker) routine helps structure observation, but business decisions should also reflect lead quality and sales progress.

## How should LinkedIn support a refresh without adding more noise?

An updated article is one asset in a broader system. If your audience uses LinkedIn, use the refresh to share a question, explain a finding, and give readers a reason to explore further. Do not copy the article into a post or present every technical correction as breaking news. A short field observation, an important caveat, or an anonymized example can show why the topic matters. The [B2B LinkedIn approach](https://extrimify.com/en/linkedin-b2b) explains how a professional presence can support positioning and the buyer journey, rather than chasing reactions for their own sake.

Give each post a clear purpose: invite a useful question, start a conversation with a relevant account, or help a buyer assess fit. When the reader reaches the page, the promise should match the post, the explanation should be consistent, and the next action should be appropriate. If the page discusses a problem your organization does not solve, do not try to conceal that mismatch with SEO edits. Change the message, choose a more relevant audience, or stop investing in that page. That is how content maintenance supports go-to-market work instead of becoming an isolated editing ritual.

## When is a refresh not enough, and how do you know the system is improving?

Refreshing will not solve a page with no defined audience, an unclear offer, weak evidence, or no connection to a relevant next step. Adding paragraphs can hide those problems without fixing them. Talk with sales and customers, test the assumptions, and decide whether to revise the page, create a different asset, or stop promoting the topic. If the page needs strategic repositioning, align that work with the wider go-to-market plan before changing the copy.

Success is not merely appearing in an AI answer. Look for a combination of signals: the page answers its intended question more clearly, relevant people reach it, they take the intended next step, and sales conversations start with better context. Keep the earlier version, change date, and reason for each revision so the team can learn across cycles. If impressions improve but lead quality does not, revisit the match between topic and offer. If users engage but do not convert, inspect the page experience and the next action.

### FAQ

#### Should every page be refreshed every 60 to 90 days?

No. This is a possible planning window for priority pages, not a site-wide rule. Update when evidence, customer questions, or business changes reveal a meaningful gap. Leave accurate, useful pages alone when an edit would add no value.

#### Does refreshing content guarantee AI citations?

No. Neither a citation nor a ranking is guaranteed by an update. A substantive refresh can maintain accuracy and usefulness, while repeated observation helps you understand visibility alongside traffic and qualified inquiries.

#### What should a team check in the first week?

Choose a page and a small set of questions, document a baseline, fix one identified gap, then compare early impressions, clicks, next-step actions, and GEO observations. A week provides early signals, not proof of causation.

#### How does a content refresh contribute to B2B pipeline?

Refresh a page that answers a buyer question, connect it to a relevant offer and next step, and share a useful perspective on LinkedIn. Evaluate lead quality and sales progression as well as visibility.
